✅ True Collection Rate: ~47–50% 2025 full-year data shows a steady 47–51% collection rate every month. January 2026 has already reached 47.0% (claims almost fully settled). February is at 42.7% and still maturing. April's 26.6% is a timing artifact — not a billing problem.
✅ Kami Stevens Departure Had Upside Stevens was the highest-volume 2025 provider ($773K charged) but had the lowest collection rate: 40.2% vs a 51–53% practice average. Her exit reduced drag on overall collection performance.
⚠ Bostian Coding at 1.0% High-Complexity — $35K+ Annual Opportunity Analysis of Services Summary (Jan–Apr 2026): Bostian billed 1,260 encounters but only 12 as 99214/99215 (1.0%). She's billing 497 visits as 99213 and 493 as 99203. Under 2021 MDM guidelines, any visit where a prescription is written qualifies for 99214 — which covers the majority of UC visits. If her rate moves to 15% (conservative), that's ~177 additional 99214s per period. At ~$80 more per charge × 50% collection = ~$35K/year recovered from Bostian alone, without seeing a single additional patient.
⚠ Hurst, Hunter & Sorensen Also Undercoding 2026 YTD high-complexity rates: Hurst 6.0% (621 enc), Hunter 4.3% (254 enc), Sorensen 2.2% (272 enc). All three are well below the practice benchmark set by Dunn (30.0%) and Grise (17.5%). Combined with Bostian, correcting all four providers to a modest 15% target adds ~$50–65K/year in recovered revenue. Recommended action: audit 10–15 recent 99213-coded charts per provider and verify: (1) was a prescription written? (2) were labs/imaging ordered? (3) does the patient have chronic conditions on the problem list?
✅ Dunn & Grise Are the Coding Benchmark Emily Dunn: 30.0% high-complexity (857 enc, 257 coded 99214/99204). Murlan Grise DO: 17.5% (685 enc, 120 coded 99214/99204/99215). These two demonstrate what appropriate complexity capture looks like for this patient population. Dunn's rate is exceptional — her documentation habits should be shared as a model across the practice.
⚠ $155K Denied in 2025 — 66% Is Process-Fixable Top denial categories: Duplicates ($36.9K), Bundled/Coding ($34.8K), Missing Info ($31.3K). None of these are payer contract problems — they're internal workflow issues. Fixing claim scrubbing, auth capture, and modifier training could recover a significant portion.
⚠ Ear Wax Removal Underpaying $2,641 charged in April, only $58 collected (2.2%). Check if this is being billed under the wrong code, missing modifier, or if payer contracts need re-negotiation. Venipuncture ($1,530 charged, $19 collected) has the same issue.

💡 What the Numbers Tell You

Payroll dominates the cost structure: Staff Salaries ($378K) + Payroll Taxes ($34.6K) + Benefits ($12K) + 1099 Contractors ($10.9K) = $435.5K total people costs (68% of all costs). This is the primary lever if margins compress.

Walk-in cash income is your largest revenue driver: At $383,832 (54% of revenue), walk-in / direct-pay outpaces insurance by a significant margin — this is a strategic advantage. Insurance ($257,873 / 36%) and Membership ($45,360 / 6%) round out the mix.

Jan & Feb net looks flat — that's cash-basis timing: Annual malpractice/liability insurance ($16,944) hit in February, and large equipment purchases ($12,961) hit in January. Strip those out and both months are profitable. March ($54.5K net) and April ($44.4K net) show real operating performance.

Marketing spend dropped after January: $6,030 in January, then only $10 in February and $0 March–April — this lines up exactly with when UC volume started declining. If you restore Google Ads budget, this expense line should increase but revenue should more than offset it.
